What this section is about

y=e^x is the unique exponential that is its own derivative: at every point, gradient equals height. We review the parent graph, common transformations (including when a vertical stretch is the same as a horizontal shift), and tangents/normals to curves like y=ke^x.

\dfrac{d}{dx}e^x=e^x,\qquad e\approx 2.7183

Full theory, Examples 1–3 and a graph studio live in the Tutorial · theory page.

Prior knowledge — quick notes

Keep these in mind. For full teaching detail, open the Tutorial (or the earlier section where you first met the idea).

  • e^x is its own derivative (gradient = height); e\approx 2.718.
  • Domain all real x, range y>0; asymptote y=0 on the left.
  • Index laws with base e; \ln means \log_e.
  • Transforms, dilations-as-shifts, tangents: Tutorial.
