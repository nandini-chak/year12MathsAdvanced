The number e and y=e^x

There is only one exponential that is its own derivative. The base of that function is e\approx 2.7183, chosen so the graph has gradient 1 at the y-intercept.

\dfrac{d}{dx}e^x=e^x\qquad\text{(gradient = height)}
  • Second derivative is also e^x → always concave up.
  • Domain \mathbb R, range y>0; asymptote y=0 as x\to-\infty.
  • Inverse: \log_e x (or \ln x), with \log_e(e^x)=x and e^{\log_e x}=x (x>0).

Example 1 · Translating and reflecting

From y=e^x:

  • y=e^{-x} — reflect in the y-axis; range y>0; also a horizontal dilation with factor -1.
  • y=e^x+3 — shift up 3; asymptote y=3; range y>3.
  • y=e^{x-2} — shift right 2; also e^{-2}e^x (vertical dilation by e^{-2}).
e^xe^{-x}

Example 2 · Dilations that are also shifts

  • y=e^{3x} — horizontal dilation with factor \tfrac13.
  • y=3e^x — vertical dilation with factor 3. Because 3=e^{\ln 3}, this is also y=e^{x+\ln 3} (shift left by \ln 3).
T Teacher note
Students often miss that a positive vertical stretch of e^x is the same as a horizontal translation. Make them rewrite the coefficient as e^{\ln A}.

Example 3 · Tangent and normal

On y=2e^x at x=1: point A=(1,2e). Then y'=2e^x, so at A the gradient is 2e (equals the height).

  • Tangent: y-2e=2e(x-1)\Rightarrow y=2ex (through the origin).
  • Normal gradient -\dfrac{1}{2e}; equation x+2ey=4e^2+1.
  • Normal meets the x-axis at B=(4e^2+1,\,0); area of \triangle AOB is e(4e^2+1).
