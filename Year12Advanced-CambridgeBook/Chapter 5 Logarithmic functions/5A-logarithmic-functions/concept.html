What this section is about

The equation y=\log_e x means “x=e^y”. It is the inverse of y=e^x. We graph it, transform it like any other function, and use it to solve equations — including ones that become quadratics after a substitution.

\log_e(e^x)=x,\qquad e^{\log_e x}=x\quad (x>0)

Key features of the parent graph: domain (0,\infty), vertical asymptote x=0, intercept (1,0), gradient 1 at that point, concave down. The same function is written \ln x.

Full theory, Examples 1–6 and the interactive studio live in the Tutorial · theory page.

Prior knowledge — quick notes

Keep these in mind. For full teaching detail, open the Tutorial (or the earlier section where you first met the idea).

  • \ln x=\log_e x is the inverse of e^x; domain x>0, asymptote x=0.
  • Identities: \ln(e^x)=x and e^{\ln x}=x (x>0).
  • Change of base: \log_b x=\ln x/\ln b.
  • Graph transforms and equations: open the Tutorial.
