Natural logarithm and its graph

y=\log_e x (also \ln x) is the inverse of y=e^x. Identities (for valid domains):

\log_e(e^x)=x,\qquad e^{\log_e x}=x\quad (x>0)
  • Domain (0,\infty), range \mathbb R.
  • Vertical asymptote x=0; x-intercept (1,0).
  • Gradient at x=1 is 1; the curve is concave down.

Example 1 · Reading values from ln x

Use a calculator or identities. For instance \ln 10\approx 2.303 and \ln(0.1)=-\ln 10.

Example 2 · Simplify with log laws

\ln(e^3)=3, e^{\ln 5}=5, and \ln\frac{1}{2}=-\ln 2. Always check that arguments of \ln stay positive.

Example 3 · Graph transforms

From y=\ln x:

  • y=\ln x+1 — up 1.
  • y=\ln(x-2) — right 2 (domain x>2).
  • y=-\ln x — reflect in x-axis.
  • y=\ln(-x) — reflect in y-axis (domain x<0).

Example 4 · Change of base

For any base b>0, b\ne 1:

\log_b x=\dfrac{\ln x}{\ln b}

So \log_2 7=\dfrac{\ln 7}{\ln 2}\approx 2.807.

Example 5 · Quadratic in e^x

Let u=e^x. Then e^{2x}-5e^x+6=0 becomes u^2-5u+6=0, so u=2 or u=3 and x=\ln 2 or x=\ln 3.

Example 6 · Linear vs logarithmic scale

On a linear axis, equal steps are equal differences. On a log axis, equal steps are equal ratios — useful for sizes spanning many orders of magnitude (planets, sound intensity, pH).
