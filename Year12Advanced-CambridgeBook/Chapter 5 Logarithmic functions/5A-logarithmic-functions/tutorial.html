Review of the logarithmic function base e

Slow, step-by-step notes for low-confidence students: what \ln means, how the graph looks, how to move it, and how to solve equations.

T Teacher note
Written for very low grade / low-confidence Year 12 students. The studio uses y=A\ln(n(x-b))+c with domain n(x-b)>0. Slider values update the equation and graph together.

1 · What is ln x?

Plain words: \ln x means “natural log of x” — the power you raise e to, to get x. So if y=\ln x, then e^y=x.

\ln(e^x)=x,\qquad e^{\ln x}=x\quad (x>0)
  • Domain: only x>0 (you cannot take \ln of 0 or a negative).
  • Range: all real numbers.
  • Vertical asymptote: the line x=0 (the graph never touches the y-axis).
  • Crosses the x-axis at (1,0), because \ln 1=0.
  • At x=1 the gradient (slope) is 1. The curve bends down (concave down).
y=\ln x (dashed teal)

How to read the sketch: As x goes toward 0 from the right, y goes down to -\infty. As x gets large, y grows slowly upward.

2 · Log graph studio

Move the sliders to change y=A\ln\big(n(x-b)\big)+c. The dashed teal curve is always the parent y=\ln x.

y=\ln x transformed

y=1.5\ln x+1

Quick guide: A>1 stretches up; A<0 flips over the x-axis. Positive b shifts right. Positive c shifts up. The graph only exists where n(x-b)>0.

T Teacher note
When n<0, the graph reflects after the shift. Compare n=2 with the identity \ln(2x)=\ln x+\ln 2 (horizontal dilation = vertical shift).

Example 1 · Reading values from ln x

Method: Use the \ln button on a calculator (not log base 10, unless the question asks for \log_{10}). Round to 3 decimal places when asked.

Worked mini-examples

Step · \ln 10: calculator → about 2.302585\ldots → 2.303 (3 d.p.).

Step · \ln 0.1: notice 0.1=1/10, so \ln 0.1=\ln(1/10)=-\ln 10\approx -2.303.

Step · \ln(10^5)=5\ln 10 (power law) ≈ 5\times 2.303=11.515 → check on calculator ≈ 11.513 (book may show 11.51 / 11.52 depending on rounding of \ln 10).

Example 2 · Exact values with log laws

Formulas to keep: \ln(e^k)=k, e^{\ln a}=a (a>0), \ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln b, \ln(a/b)=\ln a-\ln b, \ln(a^n)=n\ln a.

Same method each time

1 · \ln(e^3)=3 — the ln and the e cancel; leave the power.

2 · e^{\ln 5}=5 — the e and the ln cancel; leave the inside.

3 · \ln\tfrac12=-\ln 2 — use \ln(1/a)=-\ln a.

Check: every argument of \ln must stay positive.

Example 3 · Graph transforms (with sketch)

Start from y=\ln x. Apply one change at a time:

  • y=\ln x+1 — shift up 1.
  • y=\ln(x-2) — shift right 2 (new domain x>2; asymptote moves to x=2).
  • y=-\ln x — reflect in the x-axis (flip upside down).
  • y=\ln(-x) — reflect in the y-axis (lives on the left; domain x<0).
\ln x\ln(-x)

Remember: the dashed teal curve is only drawn for x>0. The solid blue curve is only drawn for x<0. Both share the same asymptote x=0.

Example 4 · Change of base

Formula: For any allowed base b, \log_b x=\dfrac{\ln x}{\ln b}. Use this when the calculator only has \ln (or only \log_{10}).

Worked · log₂ 7

Step 1 · Write \log_2 7=\dfrac{\ln 7}{\ln 2}.

Step 2 · Calculator: \ln 7\approx 1.945910, \ln 2\approx 0.693147.

Step 3 · Divide: 1.945910\div 0.693147\approx 2.807 (3 d.p.).

Example 5 · Quadratic in e^x

Method: Let u=e^x (always positive). Rewrite every power of e in terms of u, solve the ordinary quadratic, then take x=\ln u.

Worked · e²ˣ − 5eˣ + 6 = 0

Step 1 · Put u=e^x. Then e^{2x}=(e^x)^2=u^2.

Step 2 · Equation becomes u^2-5u+6=0.

Step 3 · Factor: (u-2)(u-3)=0, so u=2 or u=3.

Step 4 · Back-substitute: e^x=2\Rightarrow x=\ln 2; e^x=3\Rightarrow x=\ln 3.

Example 6 · Linear vs logarithmic scale

On a linear axis, equal gaps mean equal differences (…, 10, 20, 30, …).

On a log axis, equal gaps mean equal ratios (…, 10, 100, 1000, …). That is useful when sizes span many orders of magnitude (planets, sound intensity, pH).
