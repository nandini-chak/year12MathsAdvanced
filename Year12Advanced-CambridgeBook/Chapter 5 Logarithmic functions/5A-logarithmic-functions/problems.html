3 · Special values Foundation

Show that \ln 1=0 and \ln e=1 using logarithm and exponential identities.

e^0=1, so by definition \ln 1=0. Also e^1=e, so \ln e=1. Alternatively e^{\ln e}=e and \log_e(e^x)=x with x=1.

4 · Solve for x Foundation

a \ln x=6 · b \ln x=-2 · c e^x=24 · d e^x=\tfrac13

  • a x=e^6
  • b x=e^{-2}
  • c x=\ln 24
  • d x=\ln\!\left(\tfrac13\right)

8 · Transform ln x Development

a up 1 · b down 2 · c horizontal stretch factor 2 · d vertical stretch factor \tfrac13

  • b y=\ln x-2

12 · Log and exponential equations Development

Solve for x (exact form).

a e^x-1=0 · b \ln x=\ln 2 · c (\ln x)^2=(\ln 3)(\ln x) · d e^x(e^x-1)=0

  • a x=0
  • b x=2 (equivalently \ln x=\ln 2)
  • c x=1 or x=3 (\ln x=0 or \ln x=\ln 3)
  • d x=0 (only root with x real; the factor e^x=0 has no solution)

13 · Mixed exponentials Development

Solve.

a e^x+e^{-x}=e^2+e^{-2} · b e^{2x}-(e^3+1)e^x+e^3=0

  • a Let u=e^x. Then u+\tfrac1u=e^2+e^{-2} → x=e or x=e^4 (Cambridge key).
  • b With u=e^x: u^2-(e^3+1)u+e^3=0 → u=1 or u=e^3 (equivalently x=0 or x=3).

15 · Planet diameters on a log scale Development

Planets are placed by diameter on a logarithmic axis. Comment on spacing and why the Sun is usually omitted.

Equal steps on a log axis represent equal ratios of diameter, so small rocky planets cluster and gas giants spread across orders of magnitude. The Sun is excluded because a log scale needs positive arguments and \ln x\to-\infty as x\to 0^+; treating the Sun would dominate or break the scale unless a separate inset or linear panel is used.

16 · Orders of magnitude Challenge

a A quantity increases from 100 to 31 600. By what factor? b How many orders of magnitude is that?

  • a Factor 316 (31600\div 100)
  • b \log_{10}(316)\approx 2.5; total orders from 100 to 31 600: \log_{10}(31600/100)\approx 5.4

17 · Sound intensity Challenge

a If intensity drops to \tfrac15 of its original value, what percentage decrease is that? b If intensity increases by a factor of 10, how many decibels is the increase?

  • a About 80\% decrease (keeping 20\%)
  • b 10\log_{10}(10)=10 dB → 10 dB increase

18 · Change-of-base equations Challenge

Solve.

a \log_4 x=3 · b \log_{10} x=\log_{10}\!\left(\tfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\right) · c \log_{1/5} x=2

  • a x=64 or \log_4 3\approx 0.792 (book lists both forms for the two related items)
  • b x=\tfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2} or \log_{10}\!\left(\tfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\right)\approx 0.209
  • c x=-1 or \log_{1/5}2\approx -0.431

19 · Sequence of transforms Challenge

Starting from y=\ln x, describe a sequence of transformations that produces y=2\ln(x+3)-1, and sketch the result.

  1. Shift left 3: y=\ln(x+3) (domain x>-3).
  2. Vertical stretch factor 2: y=2\ln(x+3).
  3. Shift down 1: y=2\ln(x+3)-1.

Asymptote moves to x=-3; passes near (-2,-1) when x+3=1.

20 · Dilation or translation? Challenge

Explain two valid interpretations of y=\ln(2x): as a horizontal dilation of y=\ln x, and as a vertical shift. Relate to \ln(2x)=\ln x+\ln 2.

Horizontal dilation: replace x by 2x, so dilation factor \tfrac12 toward the y-axis — the graph meets the axis at the same y-intercept but rises faster for x>0.

Vertical translation: log laws give \ln(2x)=\ln x+\ln 2, so the same graph is y=\ln x shifted up by \ln 2. Both descriptions are correct; they highlight the dual nature of log transforms.
