12 · Exercise 10E

The results of an experiment are known to be normal, with mean 50 and standard deviation 10. The experiment is run 600 times. a Describe what the empirical rule predicts about the data. b Using z-scores, and using a table of the standard normal, or a calculator, or statistics software, predict roughly how many scores will: lie in [−∞, 55], i lie in [35, 50], ii lie in [38, 62]. iii c For a normal distribution, values outside the interval µ −2.7σ ≤x ≤µ + 2.7σ are called outliers. Approximately, how many outliers would you expect?

Normal rule: X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2) — convert to Z, then find the probability from the standard normal table.

a About 408 scores (68% of 600) will lie within one SD from the mean, that is, in [40, 60].

About 570 scores (95% of 600) will lie within two SDs from the mean, that is, in [30, 70].

About 598 scores (99.7% of 600) will lie within three SDs from the mean, that is, in [20, 80].

b i P(X ≤55) = 0.691462/0.691462 × 600 = 415 Therefore, approximately 415 scores will lie in the interval [−∞, 55].

ii P(35 ≤X ≤50) = 0.433193/0.433193 × 600 = 260 Therefore, approximately 260 scores will lie in the interval [35, 50].

iii P(38 ≤X ≤62) = 0.769861/0.769861 × 600 = 462 Therefore, approximately 462 scores will lie in the interval [38, 62].

c x that is not in the interval µ −2.70σ ≤x ≤µ + 2.70σ is an outlier.

Hence, 50 −2.7 × 10 ≤x ≤50 + 2.7 × 10, so 23 ≤x ≤77, and all the x values that are out of this interval are outliers.

P(23 ≤X ≤77) = 0.993066 and 1 −P(23 ≤X ≤77) = 0.006934.

Therefore, 0.006934 × 600 = 4 scores are expected to be outliers.
