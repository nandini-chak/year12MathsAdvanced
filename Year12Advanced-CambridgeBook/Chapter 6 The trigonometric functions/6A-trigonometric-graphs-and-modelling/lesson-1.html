Notes to copy

Write these before the demonstration.

1. Horizontal translation

y=\sin(x-b)
  • b>0 shifts the graph right; b<0 shifts left.
  • Trap: y=\sin(x+\pi/3) is a shift left of \pi/3.
T Teacher note

Trap here means a common student mistake — not a technical maths word.

For y=\sin(x-b), positive b shifts the graph right. Students often see y=\sin(x+\pi/3) and think “plus means right.” That is wrong. Rewrite as

y=\sin\bigl(x-(-\pi/3)\bigr)

so b=-\pi/3, and the graph shifts left by \pi/3. Tell the class: the plus inside the brackets is the trap.

2. Period

The period is the smallest positive horizontal shift that maps the graph onto itself.

  • \sin x,\cos x (and sec, cosec): period 2\pi.
  • \tan x (and cot): period \pi.
  • \sin(nx),\cos(nx): period 2\pi/|n|.
  • \tan(ax): period \pi/|a|.

3. Vertical translation = mean position

y=\sin x+c

Mean position is the horizontal line y=c. Range of \sin x+c is [c-1,c+1].

4. Amplitude

y=A\sin(nx)

Amplitude is |A| for sine and cosine. Tangent has no amplitude.

T Teacher note

Quarter-period identities (board)

Period of sine is 2\pi, so a quarter-period is \pi/2.

  • \sin(x+\pi/2)=\cos x
  • \sin(x+\pi)=-\sin x
  • \sin(x+3\pi/2)=-\cos x
  • \sin(x+2\pi)=\sin x

Demo: set Family = sine, A = 1, n = 1, b = -\pi/2 (slider near −1.57) and watch the image sit on the cosine parent.

T Teacher note
Cambridge presents these as successive left shifts of the sine wave. The studio uses y=\sin(n(x-b))+c, so a left shift of \pi/2 is entered as b=-\pi/2.

Exit ticket (2 minutes)

  1. State the period and amplitude of y=3\cos 2x.
  2. Is y=\sin(x+\pi/4) a shift left or right?

Answers: (1) amplitude 3, period \pi. (2) left of \pi/4.
