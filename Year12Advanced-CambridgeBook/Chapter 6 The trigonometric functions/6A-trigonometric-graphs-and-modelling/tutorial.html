1. Horizontal translations

To slide a graph b units to the right, replace x by x-b. Positive b means right; writing x+\alpha means a left shift of \alpha.

y=\sin\!\left(x+\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right),\quad y=\cos\!\left(x-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right),\quad y=\tan\!\left(x-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)
parent sin xsin(x+π/3)

Identity check: shifting cosine right by \pi/2 gives sine: \cos(x-\pi/2)=\sin x.

T Teacher note
Matches Cambridge Example 1. Domain for the tangent case excludes the shifted asymptotes x=\pi/4+n\pi.

2. Period

The period is the smallest positive horizontal translation that maps the graph onto itself.

  • \sin x,\cos x (and sec, cosec): period 2\pi.
  • \tan x (and cot): period \pi.

Quarter-period of a sine wave is \pi/2. Shifting sine left by successive quarters produces cosine, then -\sin x, then -\cos x, then sine again.

3. Vertical translations and mean position

Replace y by y-c to shift up by c. For a wave, the mean position is the horizontal midline.

y=\sin x-3\quad\text{(mean }y=-3\text{)},\qquad y=\cos x+1\quad\text{(mean }y=1\text{)}

4. Dilations: period and amplitude

Replace x by nx to dilate horizontally by 1/|n|, so periods divide by |n|.

  • \sin nx,\cos nx: period 2\pi/|n|.
  • \tan ax: period \pi/|a|.
  • y=A\sin(nx) has amplitude |A|.

5. Combined transformations

Work with the factored form whenever possible:

y=A\cos\big(n(x-b)\big)+c

Example: y=3\cos(2x+\pi/2)-2=3\cos 2\!\left(x+\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)-2.

  1. Horizontal dilation factor 1/2 → period \pi.
  2. Vertical stretch 3 → amplitude 3.
  3. Shift left \pi/4.
  4. Shift down 2 → mean y=-2, range [-5,1].

6. Modelling and intersections

Once amplitude, period and mean are known, the same letters describe springs, tides and temperature. For equations mixing a wave with a non-trig graph, sketch both and read intersections (technology helps for approximate roots).
