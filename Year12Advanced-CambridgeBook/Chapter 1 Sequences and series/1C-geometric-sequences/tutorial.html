Theory · key ideas

A GP multiplies by the same number each step — the common ratio r.

  • Test: divide each term by the one before it. If every answer is the same number r, the list is a GP. No term may be 0, because we cannot divide by 0.
  • Next term: a_n=a_{n-1}\times r.
  • nth term: a_n=a r^{n-1}. The power is one less than the term number. The 4th term uses power 3.
  • Negative r: the sign flips every term.
  • Link: putting whole numbers into an exponential function makes a GP.
a_n=ar^{n-1}

Worked mini-examples

Example · find a and r, then a later term

Formula: r is “next ÷ previous”. Then a_n=a r^{n-1}. For the 5th term the power is 4.

Step 1 · Sequence 2, 6, 18, 54.

Step 2 · 6\div 2=3, 18\div 6=3, 54\div 18=3. The ratios match, so a=2 and r=3.

Step 3 · 3^4=81, so a_5=2\times 81=162.

Example · a negative ratio

Step 1 · Sequence 10, -30, 90.

Step 2 · -30\div 10=-3 and 90\div (-30)=-3. So a=10 and r=-3.

Step 3 · The 4th term uses power 3. (-3)^3=-27, so a_4=10\times (-27)=-270.

The signs go plus, minus, plus, minus, because r is negative.

Example · find which term

Formula: a_n=5\times 2^{n-1}. If we know the term’s value, divide by 5, then match the power of 2.

Step 1 · Suppose a_n=320. Then 5\times 2^{n-1}=320.

Step 2 · Divide both sides by 5: 2^{n-1}=64.

Step 3 · 64=2^6, so n-1=6 and n=7. The value 320 is the 7th term.
