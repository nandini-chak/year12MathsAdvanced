15 · Exercise 1C

a A piece of paper 0.1 mm thick is folded successively 100 times. How thick is it now? b A tape 1 metre long is cut in halves, and one part discarded. Then the remaining part is cut in halves, and one part discarded. This process is repeated 33 times. How long is the final piece of tape?

Formula (GP): Common ratio r=\dfrac{a_2}{a_1} (divide consecutive terms). nth term a_n=a_1 r^{{n-1}}.

a · Start with 0.1. Each term is 2 times the previous term. The sequence is: 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, . . . Hence, this is a GP with a = 0.1 and r = 2. a101 is equivalent to the thickness from 100 successive folds. an = arn−1 a101 = 0.1 \times 2100 a101 = 2100 10 mm ≑1.27 \times 1023 km ≑1.34 \times 1010 light years Note that the universe is estimated to be 13.8 billion years old. So the paper thickness is about the distance light would travel in the age of the universe. To answer tricky follow up questions from curious students: Light that reaches us today from the early universe (the CMB, as far back as we can see) was originally emitted "only" 42 million light-years away, when everything was a condensed plasma. While space expanded, that light had to travel for 13.8 billion years to reach us. That original piece of the plasma that we’re seeing now would currently be about 46 billion light-years away, due to the expansion of space. So the paper thickness is about 10% of the distance across the current observable universe.

b · After each cut, the remaining length is halved. Starting with (1) m, after 33 repetitions, L = 1 1 2 !33 m ≈1.16 \times 10−10 m ≈1.2 \times 10−10 m. This is about the size of a small atom.
