Exercise 1C

Problems with worked solutions

Attempt on paper first. Each part has its own solution button. Open one part at a time.

Two symbols to keep straight. a_n means the nth term. The n sits lower than the a. It is not the letters “an”. A GP (geometric sequence) multiplies by the same number every time. That number is the common ratio r. The first term is a. The formula is a_n=a r^{n-1}. The power is n-1, one less than the term number. The 4th term uses power 3.

T Teacher note
Parts are one per line. In the book the part letter sits under the item it labels. Fractions stay as fractions, and n is a subscript. Answers follow Chapter-1-Worked-Solutions.pdf. In Q19(b) the sixth term is 3^6, not thirty-six. In Q19(c) each term is a power of 2 multiplied by x (2^{-16}x), not 2 to the power -16x.

1 · Exercise 1C Foundation

Write out the next three terms of each sequence. They are all GPs. Each line is its own sequence.

(a) 1, 2, 4, \ldots

(b) 81, 27, 9, \ldots

(c) -7, -14, -28, \ldots

(d) -2500, -500, -100, \ldots

(e) 3, -6, 12, \ldots

(f) -25, 50, -100, \ldots

(g) 5, -5, 5, \ldots

(h) -1000, 100, -10, \ldots

(i) 0.04, 0.4, 4, \ldots

Formula: In a GP, next term = previous term × r. Find r by dividing a term by the one before it. Both divisions must give the same number.

Step 1 · \dfrac{2}{1}=2 and \dfrac{4}{2}=2. So r=2. Multiply by 2 each time.

4\times 2=8

8\times 2=16

16\times 2=32

Next three terms: 8, 16, 32.

Same method · \dfrac{27}{81}=\dfrac{1}{3} and \dfrac{9}{27}=\dfrac{1}{3}. So r=\dfrac{1}{3}. Multiply by \dfrac{1}{3} (that is the same as dividing by 3).

9\times \dfrac{1}{3}=3

3\times \dfrac{1}{3}=1

1\times \dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{1}{3}

Next three terms: 3, 1, \dfrac{1}{3}.

Same method · \dfrac{-14}{-7}=2 and \dfrac{-28}{-14}=2. So r=2.

-28\times 2=-56

-56\times 2=-112

-112\times 2=-224

Next three terms: −56, −112, −224.

Same method · \dfrac{-500}{-2500}=\dfrac{1}{5} and \dfrac{-100}{-500}=\dfrac{1}{5}. So r=\dfrac{1}{5}.

-100\times \dfrac{1}{5}=-20

-20\times \dfrac{1}{5}=-4

-4\times \dfrac{1}{5}=-\dfrac{4}{5}

Next three terms: -20, -4, -\dfrac{4}{5}.

Same method · \dfrac{-6}{3}=-2 and \dfrac{12}{-6}=-2. So r=-2. A negative ratio flips the sign each time.

12\times (-2)=-24

-24\times (-2)=48

48\times (-2)=-96

Next three terms: −24, 48, −96.

Same method · \dfrac{50}{-25}=-2 and \dfrac{-100}{50}=-2. So r=-2.

-100\times (-2)=200

200\times (-2)=-400

-400\times (-2)=800

Next three terms: 200, −400, 800.

Same method · \dfrac{-5}{5}=-1 and \dfrac{5}{-5}=-1. So r=-1. Multiplying by −1 only flips the sign.

5\times (-1)=-5

-5\times (-1)=5

5\times (-1)=-5

Next three terms: −5, 5, −5.

Same method · \dfrac{100}{-1000}=-\dfrac{1}{10} and \dfrac{-10}{100}=-\dfrac{1}{10}. So r=-\dfrac{1}{10}.

-10\times \left(-\dfrac{1}{10}\right)=1

1\times \left(-\dfrac{1}{10}\right)=-\dfrac{1}{10}

-\dfrac{1}{10}\times \left(-\dfrac{1}{10}\right)=\dfrac{1}{100}

Next three terms: 1, -\dfrac{1}{10}, \dfrac{1}{100}.

Same method · \dfrac{0.4}{0.04}=10 and \dfrac{4}{0.4}=10. So r=10.

4\times 10=40

40\times 10=400

400\times 10=4000

Next three terms: 40, 400, 4000.

a_2=1\times 3=3

a_3=3\times 3=9

a_4=9\times 3=27

4 · Exercise 1C

Use a_n=a r^{n-1} to find the fourth term of the GP with:

(a) a=5 and r=2

(b) a=300 and r=\dfrac{1}{10}

(c) a=-7 and r=2

(d) a=-64 and r=\dfrac{1}{2}

(e) a=11 and r=-2

(f) a=-15 and r=-2

Formula: a_n=a r^{n-1}. For the 4th term, n=4, so the power is 4-1=3. That is a_4=a r^{3}.

Step 1 · r^3=2^3=2\times 2\times 2=8.

Step 2 · a_4=5\times 8=40.

a_4=40.

Same formula · a_4=a r^{3}.

\left(\dfrac{1}{10}\right)^3=\dfrac{1}{1000}.

a_4=300\times \dfrac{1}{1000}=\dfrac{300}{1000}=\dfrac{3}{10}.

a_4=\dfrac{3}{10}.

Same formula · a_4=a r^{3}.

2^3=8.

a_4=-7\times 8=-56.

a_4=-56.

Same formula · a_4=a r^{3}.

\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^3=\dfrac{1}{8}.

a_4=-64\times \dfrac{1}{8}=-8.

a_4=-8.

Same formula · a_4=a r^{3}. The power 3 is odd, so a negative base stays negative.

(-2)^3=-8.

a_4=11\times (-8)=-88.

a_4=-88.

Same formula · a_4=a r^{3}.

(-2)^3=-8.

a_4=-15\times (-8)=120. Negative times negative is positive.

a_4=120.

5 · Exercise 1C

Use a_n=a r^{n-1} to write an expression for the 70th term. Leave large powers as powers.

(a) a=1 and r=3

(b) a=5 and r=7

(c) a=8 and r=-3

Formula: a_n=a r^{n-1}. For the 70th term, n=70, so the power is 70-1=69.

Step 1 · a_{70}=1\times 3^{69}=3^{69}.

a_{70}=3^{69}.

Same formula · Power is still 69.

a_{70}=5\times 7^{69}.

a_{70}=5\times 7^{69}.

Same formula · a_{70}=8\times (-3)^{69}.

69 is an odd power, so (-3)^{69}=-(3^{69}).

a_{70}=8\times \left(-(3^{69})\right)=-8\times 3^{69}.

a_{70}=-8\times 3^{69}.
