Tutorial · theory

1I · Recurring decimals and geometric series

A recurring decimal is an infinite GP. Its value is the limiting sum, written as a fraction.

T Teacher note
A dot over digits means those digits repeat. One repeating digit uses ratio \dfrac{1}{10}. Two repeating digits use ratio \dfrac{1}{100}. If some digits do not repeat, split them off before the GP.

Theory · key ideas

Every recurring decimal is a fraction. The repeating block is a GP whose ratio is a power of \dfrac{1}{10}.

  • One repeating digit. 0.\dot{3}=0.3+0.03+0.003+\cdots. First term \dfrac{3}{10}, ratio \dfrac{1}{10}.
  • Two repeating digits. 0.\dot{2}\dot{7}=0.27+0.0027+0.000027+\cdots. First term 0.27, ratio 0.01.
  • A fixed front. If some digits do not repeat, write them as an ordinary decimal, then add the GP for the tail.
  • The formula. The ratio is between 0 and 1, so S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}. Then cancel the fraction.
S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}\qquad(0<r<1)

Worked mini-examples

Example · one digit repeats

Question. Write 0.\dot{3} as a fraction in lowest terms.

Formula: S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r} when |r|<1. Here a is the first piece of the decimal and r is what you multiply by to shift one place.

Step 1 · 0.\dot{3}=0.333\ldots=0.3+0.03+0.003+\cdots.

Step 2 · a=0.3=\dfrac{3}{10} and r=\dfrac{1}{10}.

Step 3 · S_\infty=\dfrac{3/10}{1-1/10}=\dfrac{3/10}{9/10}.

Step 4 · Dividing by \dfrac{9}{10} is multiplying by \dfrac{10}{9}. \dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{10}{9}=\dfrac{3}{9}=\dfrac{1}{3}.

Answer · 0.\dot{3}=\dfrac{1}{3}.

Example · two digits repeat

Question. Write 0.\dot{2}\dot{7} as a fraction in lowest terms.

Formula: Same limiting-sum formula. Two repeating digits means the block moves two places, so r=\dfrac{1}{100}=0.01.

Step 1 · 0.\dot{2}\dot{7}=0.272727\ldots=0.27+0.0027+0.000027+\cdots.

Step 2 · a=0.27 and r=0.01.

Step 3 · S_\infty=\dfrac{0.27}{1-0.01}=\dfrac{0.27}{0.99}.

Step 4 · Multiply top and bottom by 100: \dfrac{27}{99}.

Step 5 · Divide top and bottom by 9: \dfrac{3}{11}.

Answer · 0.\dot{2}\dot{7}=\dfrac{3}{11}.

Example · some digits do not repeat

Question. Write 2.6\dot{4}\dot{5} as a fraction in lowest terms.

Formula: Split off the part that does not repeat. The tail is a GP, and you add its limiting sum to that fixed part.

Step 1 · 2.6\dot{4}\dot{5}=2.6454545\ldots=2.6+(0.045+0.00045+\cdots).

Step 2 · The GP has a=0.045 and r=0.01.

Step 3 · \dfrac{0.045}{0.99}=\dfrac{45}{990}=\dfrac{1}{22}. Check: 45\div 9=5 and 990\div 9=110, so \dfrac{45}{990}=\dfrac{5}{110}=\dfrac{1}{22}.

Step 4 · 2.6=\dfrac{26}{10}=\dfrac{13}{5}.

Step 5 · Common denominator 110: \dfrac{13}{5}=\dfrac{286}{110} and \dfrac{1}{22}=\dfrac{5}{110}.

Step 6 · \dfrac{286}{110}+\dfrac{5}{110}=\dfrac{291}{110}.

Answer · 2.6\dot{4}\dot{5}=\dfrac{291}{110}.
