Exercise 1I

Problems with worked solutions

Attempt on paper first. Each part has its own solution button. Open one part at a time.

How to read a dot. A dot over a digit means that digit repeats forever. Dots over the first and last of a block mean the whole block repeats. Cancel list for this exercise: 9=3^{2}, 99=3^{2}\times 11, 999=3^{3}\times 37, 9999=3^{2}\times 11\times 101, 99999=3^{2}\times 41\times 271, 999999=3^{3}\times 7\times 11\times 13\times 37.

T Teacher note
One button per part. In 5(g) the first 0 after the decimal point does not repeat; the block 00271 does, so the ratio is 0.00001 and the value is \dfrac{1}{3690}. Question 4(b) equals 2.8, which is \dfrac{14}{5}. Question 5(h) is 7\dfrac{27}{35}=\dfrac{272}{35}.

1 · Exercise 1I Foundation

Write each recurring decimal as an infinite GP. Then use the limiting-sum formula to write it as a fraction in lowest terms.

(a) 0.\dot{3}

(b) 0.\dot{1}

(c) 0.\dot{7}

(d) 0.\dot{6}

Formula: One repeating digit. First term a=\dfrac{\text{digit}}{10}, ratio r=\dfrac{1}{10}. Because |r|<1, S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}.

Step 1 · 0.\dot{3}=0.3+0.03+0.003+\cdots.

Step 2 · a=0.3=\dfrac{3}{10} and r=\dfrac{1}{10}.

Step 3 · 1-r=1-\dfrac{1}{10}=\dfrac{9}{10}.

Step 4 · S_\infty=\dfrac{3/10}{9/10}=\dfrac{3}{10}\times\dfrac{10}{9}=\dfrac{3}{9}.

Step 5 · Divide top and bottom by 3: \dfrac{3}{9}=\dfrac{1}{3}.

0.\dot{3}=\dfrac{1}{3}.

Formula: Same method. a=\dfrac{1}{10}, r=\dfrac{1}{10}, S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}.

Step 1 · 0.\dot{1}=0.1+0.01+0.001+\cdots.

Step 2 · 1-r=\dfrac{9}{10}.

Step 3 · S_\infty=\dfrac{1/10}{9/10}=\dfrac{1}{9}.

0.\dot{1}=\dfrac{1}{9}.

Formula: Same method. a=\dfrac{7}{10}, r=\dfrac{1}{10}.

Step 1 · 0.\dot{7}=0.7+0.07+0.007+\cdots.

Step 2 · S_\infty=\dfrac{7/10}{9/10}=\dfrac{7}{9}.

Step 3 · 7 and 9 share no common factor other than 1, so this is already in lowest terms.

0.\dot{7}=\dfrac{7}{9}.

Formula: Same method. a=\dfrac{6}{10}, r=\dfrac{1}{10}.

Step 1 · 0.\dot{6}=0.6+0.06+0.006+\cdots.

Step 2 · S_\infty=\dfrac{6/10}{9/10}=\dfrac{6}{9}.

Step 3 · Divide top and bottom by 3: \dfrac{6}{9}=\dfrac{2}{3}.

0.\dot{6}=\dfrac{2}{3}.

Write each recurring decimal as an infinite GP. Then write it as a fraction in lowest terms.

(a) 0.\dot{2}\dot{7}

(b) 0.\dot{8}\dot{1}

(c) 0.\dot{0}\dot{9}

Formula: Same method. a=\dfrac{12}{100}, r=\dfrac{1}{100}.

Formula: Same method. a=\dfrac{78}{100}, r=\dfrac{1}{100}.

Step 2 · 1-r=\dfrac{999}{1000}.

Step 3 · S_\infty=\dfrac{27}{999}.

Step 4 · 999=3^{3}\times 37 and 27=3^{3}. Divide by 27: \dfrac{27}{999}=\dfrac{1}{37}.

0.\dot{0}\dot{2}\dot{7}=\dfrac{1}{37}.

Formula: Same three-digit method. a=\dfrac{135}{1000}, r=\dfrac{1}{1000}.

Step 1 · S_\infty=\dfrac{135}{999}.

Step 2 · 135=3^{3}\times 5 and 999=3^{3}\times 37. Divide by 27: \dfrac{135}{999}=\dfrac{5}{37}.

3 · Exercise 1I Development

Split off the digits that do not repeat. The tail is an infinite GP. Add them, and write the result in lowest terms.

(a) 12.\dot{4}

(b) 7.\dot{8}\dot{1}

(c) 8.4\dot{6}

(d) 0.2\dot{3}\dot{6}

Formula: 12.\dot{4}=12+0.\dot{4}. The repeating part uses a=\dfrac{4}{10}, r=\dfrac{1}{10}, S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}.

Step 1 · 0.\dot{4}=\dfrac{4/10}{9/10}=\dfrac{4}{9}.

Step 2 · 12=\dfrac{108}{9}.

Step 3 · \dfrac{108}{9}+\dfrac{4}{9}=\dfrac{112}{9}.

12.\dot{4}=\dfrac{112}{9}.

Formula: 7.\dot{8}\dot{1}=7+0.\dot{8}\dot{1}. Two digits, so a=\dfrac{81}{100} and r=\dfrac{1}{100}.

Step 1 · From question 2(b), 0.\dot{8}\dot{1}=\dfrac{9}{11}.

Step 2 · 7=\dfrac{77}{11}.

Step 3 · \dfrac{77}{11}+\dfrac{9}{11}=\dfrac{86}{11}.

7.\dot{8}\dot{1}=\dfrac{86}{11}.

Formula: The 8.4 does not repeat. The 6 does. a=0.06=\dfrac{6}{100}, r=\dfrac{1}{10}, S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}.

Step 1 · 8.4\dot{6}=8.4+0.06+0.006+0.0006+\cdots.

Step 2 · 1-r=\dfrac{9}{10}, so the GP is \dfrac{6/100}{9/10}=\dfrac{6}{100}\times\dfrac{10}{9}=\dfrac{6}{90}=\dfrac{1}{15}.

Step 3 · 8.4=\dfrac{84}{10}=\dfrac{42}{5}=\dfrac{126}{15}.

Step 4 · \dfrac{126}{15}+\dfrac{1}{15}=\dfrac{127}{15}=8\dfrac{7}{15}.

8.4\dot{6}=\dfrac{127}{15}.

Formula: The 0.2 does not repeat. The block 36 does. a=0.036=\dfrac{36}{1000}, r=\dfrac{1}{100}.

Step 1 · 0.2\dot{3}\dot{6}=0.2+0.036+0.00036+\cdots.

Step 2 · 1-r=\dfrac{99}{100}, so the GP is \dfrac{36/1000}{99/100}=\dfrac{36}{1000}\times\dfrac{100}{99}=\dfrac{36}{990}=\dfrac{2}{55}.

Step 3 · 0.2=\dfrac{1}{5}=\dfrac{11}{55}.

Step 4 · \dfrac{11}{55}+\dfrac{2}{55}=\dfrac{13}{55}.

0.2\dot{3}\dot{6}=\dfrac{13}{55}.

4 · Exercise 1I Development

(a) Write 0.\dot{9} as an infinite GP, and show that it equals 1.

(b) Write 2.7\dot{9} as 2.7 plus an infinite GP, and show that it equals 2.8.

Formula: One repeating digit. a=\dfrac{9}{10}, r=\dfrac{1}{10}, S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}.

Step 1 · 0.\dot{9}=0.9+0.09+0.009+\cdots.

Step 2 · S_\infty=\dfrac{9/10}{9/10}=1.

0.\dot{9}=1.

Formula: 2.7\dot{9}=2.7+0.09+0.009+\cdots. The GP has a=0.09 and r=\dfrac{1}{10}.

Step 1 · 1-r=\dfrac{9}{10}.

Step 2 · GP sum =\dfrac{0.09}{0.9}=0.1.

Step 3 · 2.7+0.1=2.8.

Step 4 · As a fraction, 2.8=\dfrac{28}{10}=\dfrac{14}{5}.

2.7\dot{9}=2.8=\dfrac{14}{5}.

5 · Exercise 1I Challenge

Use GPs to write these as fractions in lowest terms.

(a) 0.\dot{0}\dot{9}\dot{5}\dot{7}

(b) 0.\dot{2}\dot{4}\dot{7}\dot{5}

(c) 0.\dot{2}\dot{3}\dot{0}\dot{7}\dot{6}\dot{9}

(d) 0.\dot{4}\dot{2}\dot{8}\dot{5}\dot{7}\dot{1}

(e) 0.25\dot{5}\dot{7}

(f) 1.1\dot{0}\dot{3}\dot{7}

(g) 0.0\dot{0}\dot{0}\dot{2}\dot{7}\dot{1}

(h) 7.7\dot{7}\dot{1}\dot{4}\dot{2}\dot{8}\dot{5}

Formula: Four repeating digits. a=\dfrac{957}{10000}, r=\dfrac{1}{10000}, S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}.

Step 1 · 1-r=\dfrac{9999}{10000}, so S_\infty=\dfrac{957}{9999}.

Step 2 · 957=3\times 11\times 29 and 9999=3^{2}\times 11\times 101.

Step 3 · Cancel 3\times 11: \dfrac{957}{9999}=\dfrac{29}{303}.

0.\dot{0}\dot{9}\dot{5}\dot{7}=\dfrac{29}{303}.

Formula: Same four-digit method. a=\dfrac{2475}{10000}, r=\dfrac{1}{10000}.

Step 1 · S_\infty=\dfrac{2475}{9999}.

Step 2 · 2475=3^{2}\times 5^{2}\times 11 and 9999=3^{2}\times 11\times 101.

Step 3 · Cancel 3^{2}\times 11: \dfrac{2475}{9999}=\dfrac{25}{101}.

0.\dot{2}\dot{4}\dot{7}\dot{5}=\dfrac{25}{101}.

Formula: Six repeating digits. a=\dfrac{230769}{1000000}, r=\dfrac{1}{1000000}, so S_\infty=\dfrac{230769}{999999}.

Step 1 · 999999=3^{3}\times 7\times 11\times 13\times 37.

Step 2 · 230769=3^{4}\times 7\times 11\times 37. Check: 3^{4}=81, 81\times 7=567, 567\times 11=6237, 6237\times 37=230769.

Step 3 · Cancel 3^{3}\times 7\times 11\times 37. Left on top: 3. Left on the bottom: 13.

0.\dot{2}\dot{3}\dot{0}\dot{7}\dot{6}\dot{9}=\dfrac{3}{13}.

Formula: Same six-digit method. S_\infty=\dfrac{428571}{999999}.

Step 1 · 999999=3^{3}\times 7\times 11\times 13\times 37.

Step 2 · 428571=3^{4}\times 11\times 13\times 37. Check: 81\times 11=891, 891\times 13=11583, 11583\times 37=428571.

Step 3 · Cancel 3^{3}\times 11\times 13\times 37. Left on top: 3. Left on the bottom: 7.

0.\dot{4}\dot{2}\dot{8}\dot{5}\dot{7}\dot{1}=\dfrac{3}{7}.

Formula: 0.25 does not repeat. The block 57 does. a=0.0057=\dfrac{57}{10000}, r=\dfrac{1}{100}.

Step 1 · 0.25\dot{5}\dot{7}=0.25+(0.0057+0.000057+\cdots).

Step 2 · GP sum =\dfrac{57/10000}{99/100}=\dfrac{57}{9900}=\dfrac{19}{3300}.

Step 3 · 0.25=\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{825}{3300}.

Step 4 · \dfrac{825}{3300}+\dfrac{19}{3300}=\dfrac{844}{3300}.

Step 5 · 844=2^{2}\times 211 and 3300=2^{2}\times 3\times 5^{2}\times 11. Cancel 4: \dfrac{844}{3300}=\dfrac{211}{825}.

0.25\dot{5}\dot{7}=\dfrac{211}{825}.

Formula: 1.1 does not repeat. The block 037 does. a=0.0037=\dfrac{37}{10000}, r=\dfrac{1}{1000}.

Step 1 · 1.1\dot{0}\dot{3}\dot{7}=1.1+(0.0037+0.0000037+\cdots).

Step 2 · GP sum =\dfrac{37/10000}{999/1000}=\dfrac{37}{9990}.

Step 3 · 1.1=\dfrac{11}{10}=\dfrac{11\times 999}{9990}=\dfrac{10989}{9990}.

Step 4 · \dfrac{10989}{9990}+\dfrac{37}{9990}=\dfrac{11026}{9990}.

Step 5 · 11026=2\times 37\times 149 and 9990=2\times 3^{3}\times 5\times 37. Cancel 2\times 37: \dfrac{11026}{9990}=\dfrac{149}{135}.

Step 6 · 149=135+14, so \dfrac{149}{135}=1\dfrac{14}{135}.

1.1\dot{0}\dot{3}\dot{7}=\dfrac{149}{135}=1\dfrac{14}{135}.

Formula: The first 0 after the point does not repeat. The block 00271 does (five digits). a=0.000271, r=0.00001, S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}.

Step 1 · 0.0\dot{0}\dot{0}\dot{2}\dot{7}\dot{1}=0.0002710027100271\ldots.

Step 2 · 1-r=0.99999, so the sum is \dfrac{0.000271}{0.99999}=\dfrac{271/1000000}{99999/100000}.

Step 3 · =\dfrac{271}{1000000}\times\dfrac{100000}{99999}=\dfrac{271}{999990}.

Step 4 · 99999=3^{2}\times 41\times 271, so 999990=10\times 3^{2}\times 41\times 271.

Step 5 · Cancel 271: \dfrac{271}{999990}=\dfrac{1}{3690}. Check: 10\times 9\times 41=3690.

0.0\dot{0}\dot{0}\dot{2}\dot{7}\dot{1}=\dfrac{1}{3690}.

Formula: 7.7 does not repeat. The block 714285 does (six digits). a=0.0714285, r=0.000001.

Step 1 · 7.7\dot{7}\dot{1}\dot{4}\dot{2}\dot{8}\dot{5}=7.7+(0.0714285+0.0000000714285+\cdots).

Step 2 · GP sum =\dfrac{0.0714285}{0.999999}=\dfrac{714285/10000000}{999999/1000000}=\dfrac{714285}{9999990}.

Step 3 · \dfrac{714285}{999999}=\dfrac{5}{7}, because 5\times 999999=4999995 and 7\times 714285=4999995.

Step 4 · So the GP is \dfrac{5}{7}\div 10=\dfrac{5}{70}=\dfrac{1}{14}.

Step 5 · 7.7=\dfrac{77}{10}=\dfrac{539}{70} and \dfrac{1}{14}=\dfrac{5}{70}.

Step 6 · \dfrac{539}{70}+\dfrac{5}{70}=\dfrac{544}{70}=\dfrac{272}{35}.

Step 7 · 272=7\times 35+27, so \dfrac{272}{35}=7\dfrac{27}{35}.

7.7\dot{7}\dot{1}\dot{4}\dot{2}\dot{8}\dot{5}=7\dfrac{27}{35}.

6 · Exercise 1I Challenge

Last year it was proved that \sqrt{2} is irrational. Why does that mean the decimal for \sqrt{2} is not a recurring decimal?

Quick fact: Irrational means “not a fraction of two whole numbers”. Every recurring decimal in this section became such a fraction.

Step 1 · \sqrt{2} is irrational, so it is not equal to \dfrac{p}{q} for whole numbers p and q.

Step 2 · If its decimal recurred, the method of this section would turn that decimal into a fraction \dfrac{p}{q}.

Step 3 · That fraction would equal \sqrt{2}, which cannot happen.

The decimal for \sqrt{2} does not recur.
