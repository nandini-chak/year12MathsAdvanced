Exercise 1B

Problems with worked solutions

Attempt on paper first. Each part has its own solution button. Open one part at a time.

Two symbols to keep straight. a_n means the nth term. The n sits lower than the a. It is not the letters “an”. An AP (arithmetic sequence) adds or subtracts the same number every time. That number is the common difference d. The first term is a. The formula is a_n=a+(n-1)d.

1 · Exercise 1B Foundation

Write the next three terms of each sequence. They are all APs.

(a) 2, 6, 10, \ldots

(b) 3, 8, 13, \ldots

(c) 35, 25, 15, \ldots

(d) 11, 5, -1, \ldots

(e) 4\tfrac{1}{2},\; 6,\; 7\tfrac{1}{2},\; \ldots

(f) 8,\; 7\tfrac{1}{2},\; 7,\; \ldots

Quick fact: In an AP, d = second term − first term. Then keep adding d. Check the same jump on the next pair before you continue.

d=6-2=4, and 10-6=4. Add 4 each time.

10+4=14

14+4=18

18+4=22

Next three terms: 14, 18, 22.

Same method · d=8-3=5, and 13-8=5. Add 5 each time.

13+5=18

18+5=23

23+5=28

Next three terms: 18, 23, 28.

Same method · d=25-35=-10, and 15-25=-10. Subtract 10 each time.

15+(-10)=5

5+(-10)=-5

-5+(-10)=-15

Next three terms: 5, −5, −15.

Same method · d=5-11=-6, and -1-5=-6. Subtract 6 each time.

-1-6=-7

-7-6=-13

-13-6=-19

Next three terms: −7, −13, −19.

Same method · 4\tfrac{1}{2}=\tfrac{9}{2} and 7\tfrac{1}{2}=\tfrac{15}{2}.

d=6-\tfrac{9}{2}=\tfrac{12}{2}-\tfrac{9}{2}=\tfrac{3}{2}, and \tfrac{15}{2}-6=\tfrac{15}{2}-\tfrac{12}{2}=\tfrac{3}{2}. Add 1\tfrac{1}{2} each time.

7\tfrac{1}{2}+1\tfrac{1}{2}=9

9+1\tfrac{1}{2}=10\tfrac{1}{2}

10\tfrac{1}{2}+1\tfrac{1}{2}=12

Next three terms: 9,\; 10\tfrac{1}{2},\; 12.

Same method · d=7\tfrac{1}{2}-8=-\tfrac{1}{2}, and 7-7\tfrac{1}{2}=-\tfrac{1}{2}. Subtract \tfrac{1}{2} each time.

7-\tfrac{1}{2}=6\tfrac{1}{2}

6\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}=6

6-\tfrac{1}{2}=5\tfrac{1}{2}

Next three terms: 6\tfrac{1}{2},\; 6,\; 5\tfrac{1}{2}.

a_4=7+2=9

7-4=3

3-4=-1

-1-4=-5

17+11=28

28+11=39

39+11=50

17, 28, 39, 50.

30-11=19

-5+4=-1

-1+4=3

−9, −5, −1, 3.

4\tfrac{1}{2},\; 4,\; 3\tfrac{1}{2},\; 3.

1.6+0.7=2.3

a_3-a_2=11-7=4

a_3-a_2=24-17=7

It is an AP with a=10 and d=7.

a_3-a_2=40-20=20

10 and 20 are not the same, so the jump is not constant.

Not an AP.

Same method · a_2-a_1=35-50=-15

Same method · a_2-a_1=34-23=11

a_3-a_2=45-34=11

4 · Exercise 1B

Use a_n=a+(n-1)d to find the 11th term a_{11}.

(a) a=7 and d=6

(b) a=15 and d=-7

(c) a=10\tfrac{1}{2} and d=4

Formula: a_n=a+(n-1)d. Here a is the first term, d is the common difference, and n is the term number. For the 11th term, n-1=10, so we add d ten times.

a_n=7+6(n-1)

Expand the bracket: 6(n-1)=6n-6, so a_n=7+6n-6=6n+1.

Put n=11: a_{11}=6\times 11+1=66+1=67.

Check with the short form: a_{11}=7+10\times 6=7+60=67.

a_{11}=67.

Same formula · a_n=15+(-7)(n-1)=15-7(n-1).

Expand: -7(n-1)=-7n+7, so a_n=15-7n+7=22-7n.

Put n=11: a_{11}=22-7\times 11=22-77=-55.

Check: a_{11}=15+10\times(-7)=15-70=-55.

a_{11}=-55.

Same formula · a=10\tfrac{1}{2}=\tfrac{21}{2} and d=4.

a_n=\tfrac{21}{2}+4(n-1)=\tfrac{21}{2}+4n-4.

4=\tfrac{8}{2}, so \tfrac{21}{2}-4=\tfrac{21}{2}-\tfrac{8}{2}=\tfrac{13}{2}.

a_n=4n+\tfrac{13}{2}.

Put n=11: a_{11}=4\times 11+\tfrac{13}{2}=44+\tfrac{13}{2}=\tfrac{88}{2}+\tfrac{13}{2}=\tfrac{101}{2}=50\tfrac{1}{2}.

a_{11}=50\tfrac{1}{2}.

6 · Exercise 1B

The AP is 6, 16, 26, \ldots

(a) Find the first term a and the common difference d.

(b) Find a_9, a_{21} and a_{100}.

(c) Find a formula for a_n.

The first term is the first number in the list: a=6.

a_2-a_1=16-6=10.

a_3-a_2=26-16=10. The jumps match, so d=10.

a=6 and d=10.

Formula: a_n=a+(n-1)d=6+10(n-1). The (n-1) means “how many jumps from the first term”.

a_9=6+10(9-1)=6+10\times 8=6+80=86.

a_{21}=6+10(21-1)=6+10\times 20=6+200=206.

a_{100}=6+10(100-1)=6+10\times 99=6+990=996.

a_9=86, a_{21}=206, a_{100}=996.

a_n=6+10(n-1).

Expand: 10(n-1)=10n-10, so a_n=6+10n-10=10n-4.

a_n=10n-4.

10 · Exercise 1B

The sequence is 165, 160, 155, \ldots

(a) Find a formula for a_n.

(b) The finite list ends at 40. How many terms are there?

(c) Find the first negative term of 165, 160, 155, \ldots

a_2-a_1=160-165=-5 and a_3-a_2=155-160=-5. So a=165 and d=-5.

a_n=165-5(n-1)=165-5n+5=170-5n.

a_n=170-5n.

40=170-5n.

Add 5n to both sides: 40+5n=170.

Subtract 40: 5n=130.

Divide by 5: n=26.

Check: a_{26}=170-5\times 26=170-130=40.

We want a_n<0, so 170-5n<0.

Divide by 5: n>34.

13 · Exercise 1B

The nth term is a_n=7+4n.

(a) Write the first four terms. Then find a and d.

(b) Find the sum of the 50th and 25th terms, and also their difference.

(c) Show that 5a_1+4a_2=a_{27}.

(d) Which term is 815?

(e) Find the last term less than 1000, and the first term greater than 1000.

(f) Which terms lie between 200 and 300, and how many are there?

Quick fact: Replace n by 1, then 2, then 3, then 4. The first term is a_1, not the 7 sitting in the formula by itself.

a_1=7+4\times 1=11

a_2=7+4\times 2=15

a_3=7+4\times 3=19

a_4=7+4\times 4=23

d=15-11=4. The first term is 11.

11, 15, 19, 23. So a=11 and d=4.

a_{25}=7+4\times 25=7+100=107.

a_{50}=7+4\times 50=7+200=207.

Sum: 107+207=314.

Difference: 207-107=100.

Sum 314. Difference 100.

Work out the left side and the right side separately, then compare them.

Left: 5a_1+4a_2=5\times 11+4\times 15=55+60=115.

Right: a_{27}=7+4\times 27=7+108=115.

Both sides equal 115, so 5a_1+4a_2=a_{27}.

Put a_n=815: 815=7+4n.

Subtract 7: 808=4n.

Divide by 4: n=202.

Check: 7+4\times 202=7+808=815.

815 is the 202nd term.

1000 is not a term. Solve 7+4n=1000 to see where it would sit: 4n=993, n=\tfrac{993}{4}=248\tfrac{1}{4}.

a_{249}=7+4\times 249=7+996=1003, which is greater than 1000.
