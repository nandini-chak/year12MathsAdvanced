Exercise 1D

Problems with worked solutions

Attempt on paper first. Each part has its own solution button. Open one part at a time.

Two tests, and one symbol. a_n means the nth term. The n sits lower than the a. It is not the letters “an”. An AP adds the same gap every time. For three numbers, second − first = third − second. A GP multiplies by the same number every time. For three non-zero numbers, second ÷ first = third ÷ second. If the middle number is the unknown, its square equals first × third.

1 · Exercise 1D Foundation

Find x so that each set of three numbers is an arithmetic sequence (an AP). The gap must stay the same.

(a) 5,\; x,\; 17

(b) 32,\; x,\; 14

(c) -12,\; x,\; -50

(d) -23,\; x,\; 7

(e) x,\; 22,\; 32

(f) -20,\; -5,\; x

Formula: Three numbers form an AP when the gap stays the same. Second minus first equals third minus second: a_2-a_1=a_3-a_2. The small number under a is which term it is. It is not the letters “an”.

Step 1 · The three numbers are 5,\; x,\; 17.

Step 2 · Equal gaps. Second minus first equals third minus second. x-5=17-x.

Step 3 · Add x to both sides. 2x-5=17.

Step 4 · Add 5 to both sides. 2x=22.

Step 5 · Divide both sides by 2. x=11.

x=11. Check: 11-5=6 and 17-11=6. Both gaps are 6.

Same method · An AP has one unchanging gap. Second − first = third − second.

Step 1 · The three numbers are 32,\; x,\; 14.

Step 2 · Equal gaps. x-32=14-x.

Step 3 · Add x to both sides. 2x-32=14.

Step 4 · Add 32. 2x=46.

Step 5 · Divide by 2. x=23.

x=23. Check: 23-32=-9 and 14-23=-9.

Same method · An AP has one unchanging gap. Second − first = third − second.

Step 1 · The three numbers are -12,\; x,\; -50.

Step 2 · Equal gaps. Subtracting a negative is the same as adding. x-(-12)=(-50)-x.

Step 3 · That simplifies to x+12=-50-x.

Step 4 · Add x. 2x+12=-50.

Step 5 · Subtract 12. 2x=-62.

Step 6 · Divide by 2. x=-31.

x=-31. Check: -31-(-12)=-19 and -50-(-31)=-19.

Same method · An AP has one unchanging gap. Second − first = third − second.

Step 1 · The three numbers are -23,\; x,\; 7.

Step 2 · Equal gaps. x-(-23)=7-x.

Step 3 · Simplify the left side. x+23=7-x.

Step 4 · Add x. 2x+23=7.

Step 5 · Subtract 23. 2x=-16.

Step 6 · Divide by 2. x=-8.

x=-8. Check: -8-(-23)=15 and 7-(-8)=15.

Same method · An AP has one unchanging gap. Second − first = third − second.

Step 1 · The three numbers are x,\; 22,\; 32.

Step 2 · This time the unknown is the first term. 22-x=32-22.

Step 3 · Work out the known gap. 22-x=10.

Step 4 · Add x and subtract 10. 22-10=x.

Step 5 · So x=12.

x=12. Check: 22-12=10 and 32-22=10.

Same method · An AP has one unchanging gap. Second − first = third − second.

Step 1 · The three numbers are -20,\; -5,\; x.

Step 2 · The unknown is the third term. -5-(-20)=x-(-5).

Step 3 · Simplify both sides. 15=x+5.

Step 4 · Subtract 5. x=10.

2 · Exercise 1D Foundation

Each triple is a geometric sequence (a GP). Find x. None of these terms is zero.

(a) 2,\; x,\; 18

(b) 48,\; x,\; 3

(c) -10,\; x,\; -90

(d) -98,\; x,\; -2

(e) x,\; 20,\; 80

(f) -1,\; 4,\; x

Formula: Three non-zero numbers form a GP when the multiplier stays the same. Second ÷ first equals third ÷ second: \dfrac{a_2}{a_1}=\dfrac{a_3}{a_2}. That is the same as a_2^{2}=a_1 a_3 when the middle term is the unknown.

Step 1 · The three numbers are 2,\; x,\; 18.

Step 2 · The middle term is unknown, so its square equals first × third. x^{2}=2\times 18.

Step 3 · Work out the product. x^{2}=36.

Step 4 · Both 6 and −6 square to 36, because a negative times a negative is positive. x=6\;\text{or}\;x=-6.

x=6\;\text{or}\;x=-6. Check: Both triples work: 2, 6, 18 multiplies by 3, and 2, −6, 18 multiplies by −3.

Same method · A GP multiplies by the same number each time. If the middle term is unknown, its square equals first × third.

Step 1 · The three numbers are 48,\; x,\; 3.

Step 2 · Middle squared equals first × third. x^{2}=48\times 3.

Step 3 · Work out the product. x^{2}=144.

Step 4 · 12 and −12 both square to 144. x=12\;\text{or}\;x=-12.

x=12\;\text{or}\;x=-12. Check: 48, 12, 3 multiplies by \dfrac{1}{4}. 48, −12, 3 multiplies by -\dfrac{1}{4}.

Same method · A GP multiplies by the same number each time. If the middle term is unknown, its square equals first × third.

Step 1 · The three numbers are -10,\; x,\; -90.

Step 2 · Middle squared equals first × third. x^{2}=(-10)\times(-90).

Step 3 · A negative times a negative is positive. x^{2}=900.

Step 4 · 30 and −30 both square to 900. x=30\;\text{or}\;x=-30.

x=30\;\text{or}\;x=-30. Check: −10, 30, −90 multiplies by −3. −10, −30, −90 multiplies by 3.

Same method · A GP multiplies by the same number each time. If the middle term is unknown, its square equals first × third.

Step 1 · The three numbers are -98,\; x,\; -2.

Step 2 · Middle squared equals first × third. x^{2}=(-98)\times(-2).

Step 3 · Work out the product. x^{2}=196.

Step 4 · 14 and −14 both square to 196. x=14\;\text{or}\;x=-14.

x=14\;\text{or}\;x=-14. Check: Both triples multiply by a constant ratio.

Same method · A GP multiplies by the same number each time. If the middle term is unknown, its square equals first × third.

Step 1 · The three numbers are x,\; 20,\; 80.

Step 2 · The unknown is the first term, so use the ratio, not a square. \dfrac{20}{x}=\dfrac{80}{20}.

Step 3 · The right-hand ratio is \dfrac{80}{20}=4.

Step 4 · So \dfrac{20}{x}=4.

Step 5 · Multiply both sides by x, then divide by 4. 20=4x.

Step 6 · Divide by 4. x=5.

x=5. Check: Only one answer. A negative x would make \dfrac{20}{x} negative, but 4 is positive. Check: \dfrac{20}{5}=4 and \dfrac{80}{20}=4.

Same method · A GP multiplies by the same number each time. If the middle term is unknown, its square equals first × third.

Step 1 · The three numbers are -1,\; 4,\; x.

Step 2 · The unknown is the third term. \dfrac{4}{-1}=\dfrac{x}{4}.

Step 3 · Simplify the left side. -4=\dfrac{x}{4}.

Step 4 · Multiply both sides by 4. x=-16.

x=-16. Check: \dfrac{4}{-1}=-4 and \dfrac{-16}{4}=-4.
