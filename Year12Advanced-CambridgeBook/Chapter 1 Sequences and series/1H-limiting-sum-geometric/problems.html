How to read S_\infty. S_\infty means the limiting sum: the number the running totals get close to if you keep adding forever. Use S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r} only when the size of the multiplier is less than 1, written |r|<1. If |r|\ge 1, say there is no limiting sum, and say why the terms do not die away.

7 · Exercise 1H Foundation

Bevin drops a bouncy ball from a height of 8 metres. Every bounce reaches half the height of the bounce before it.

(a) Show that the first down-and-up trip is 12 metres, and explain why the later down-and-up trips form a geometric sequence with r=\dfrac{1}{2}.

(b) How far does the ball travel in the end?

Quick fact: A down-and-up trip is the fall to the ground plus the rise back up. A geometric sequence multiplies by the same number every time. That number is r.

Step 1 · The ball falls 8 metres to the ground.

Step 2 · It rises half of 8, which is 4 metres.

Step 3 · Down plus up: 8+4=12 metres. That is the first down-and-up distance.

Step 4 · The next bounce starts from 4 metres. It falls 4 metres and rises half of 4, which is 2 metres. That trip is 4+2=6 metres.

Step 5 · 6 is half of 12. Each new height is half the one before, so each new down-and-up trip is half the previous trip.

The first down-and-up distance is 12 metres, and the trips form a geometric sequence with r=\dfrac{1}{2}.

Formula: The down-and-up distances are a geometric series with a=12 and r=\dfrac{1}{2}. Because |r|<1, S_\infty=\dfrac{a}{1-r}. “In the end” means this limiting sum.

Step 1 · 1-r=1-\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}.

Step 2 · S_\infty=\dfrac{12}{\frac{1}{2}}=12\times 2=24.

The ball travels 24 metres in the end.
