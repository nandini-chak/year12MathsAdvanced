How to read S_n and \sum. S_n means the sum of the first n terms. The small n sits lower than the S. It is not the letters “Sn”. The sign \sum means “add”. The number under it is where you start. The number above it is where you stop.

Step 2 · 3+5=8.

Step 3 · 8+7=15.

Step 4 · 15+9=24.

S_4=24.

Step 2 · 2+6=8.

Step 3 · 8+18=26.

Step 4 · 26+54=80.

S_4=80.

Step 1 · The first four terms are 6, 2, −2 and −6.

Step 2 · 6+2=8.

Step 3 · 8+(-2)=6.

Step 4 · 6+(-6)=0.

Formula: Same method. Write the fractions with denominator 4 before you add.

Step 1 · The first four terms are 2, 1, \dfrac{1}{2} and \dfrac{1}{4}.

Quick fact: The derivative of e^{x} is e^{x} again. Taking differences of a sequence is the discrete cousin of differentiating.

Step 1 · Drop the opening 2 from the list in part (b). The remaining terms are 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64.

Quick fact: The derivative of x^{3} is 3x^{2}, a quadratic. The derivative of 3x^{2} is 6x, a straight-line formula.

Step 1 · The sums are the cubes n^{3}.

Step 2 · One set of differences gives a_n=3n^{2}-3n+1, which is quadratic, like 3x^{2}.

Step 3 · A second set of differences gives 6,\;12,\;18,\;24,\;\ldots, which goes up by 6 each time, like 6x.

Taking differences once turns the cubes into a quadratic list. Taking differences again turns that into a linear list. That matches differentiating x^{3} twice.
