1E · Adding up the terms of a sequence

Direct path: Concept → Tutorial → Exercise → next section. No extra hubs.

What this section is about

A series means we add the terms of a sequence.

  • Picture it: a stone falls 5+15+25 metres in three seconds. That total, 45 m, is a sum.
  • Partial sum S_n: add the first n terms. S_3=5+15+25=45.
  • Sigma: \sum is a short way to write “add these”. The worked reading of the sign is in the Tutorial.
  • Word tip: sequence = the list. Series = the list added up.

Full detail and examples live in the Tutorial · theory — this page only sets the scene.

Prior knowledge — quick notes

Keep these in mind. For full teaching detail, open the Tutorial (or the earlier section where you first met the idea).

  • A sequence is an ordered list. a_1 is the first term. a_n is the nth term. The small n is not the letters “an”.
  • An AP adds the same gap each time. A GP multiplies by the same number each time.
  • You can add a short list by writing the numbers in a row: 5+15+25=45.
  • Worked steps for partial sums and sigma are in the Tutorial.
