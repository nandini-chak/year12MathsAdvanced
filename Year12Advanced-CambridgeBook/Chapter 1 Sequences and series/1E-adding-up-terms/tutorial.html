Tutorial · theory

1E · Adding up the terms of a sequence

A series is the list added up. This page shows how to build a partial sum, how to get the list back from the sums, and how to read a sigma sign.

T Teacher note
Each equation is on its own line. The sigma symbol is typeset, with the start number under the sign and the stop number above it. Closed formulae for AP and GP sums are left for 1F and 1G.

Theory · key ideas

A sequence is the list. A series is that list added up. S_n means the sum of the first n terms.

  • Partial sum. S_n=a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n. It is also called the sum of the first n terms, or the sum to n terms.
  • The next sum. Keep the sum you already have, and add the next term: S_n=S_{n-1}+a_n.
  • Back to the list. The first term is the first sum: a_1=S_1. Every later term is a gap between two sums: a_n=S_n-S_{n-1} for n\ge 2.
  • Sigma. The sign \sum means “add these”. The small number underneath is where you start. The number on top is where you stop. The letter underneath is a counter. It disappears once you have added.
  • Later. Sections 1F and 1G give short formulae for an AP sum and a GP sum. This section adds the terms one by one.
S_n=a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n
a_1=S_1,\qquad a_n=S_n-S_{n-1}\ \text{for}\ n\ge 2
\displaystyle S_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_k

Worked mini-examples

Example · read a partial sum

Question. A stone falls 5 m in the first second, 15 m in the next, then 25 m, then 35 m. Find S_3 and S_4.

Formula: S_n is the sum of the first n terms. The next partial sum is the one you have plus the next term: S_n=S_{n-1}+a_n.

Step 1 · The first three distances are 5, 15 and 25.

Step 2 · S_3=5+15+25.

Step 3 · 5+15=20, and 20+25=45. So S_3=45.

Step 4 · The fourth distance is 35. S_4=S_3+35=45+35=80.

Answer · In 3 seconds the stone has fallen 45 m. In 4 seconds it has fallen 80 m.

Example · fill the partial-sum row

Question. The terms are 5,\;15,\;25,\;35,\;45. Write S_1 to S_5.

Formula: S_1=a_1. After that, S_n=S_{n-1}+a_n.

Step 1 · S_1=5.

Step 2 · S_2=5+15=20.

Step 3 · S_3=20+25=45.

Step 4 · S_4=45+35=80.

Step 5 · S_5=80+45=125.

Answer · The partial sums are 5, 20, 45, 80, 125.

Example · get the list back from the sums

Question. The partial sums are S_1=1, S_2=5, S_3=12, S_4=22. Write a_1 to a_4.

Formula: a_1=S_1. For n\ge 2, a_n=S_n-S_{n-1}. You are finding the gap between two neighbouring sums.

Step 1 · a_1=S_1=1.

Step 2 · a_2=S_2-S_1=5-1=4.

Step 3 · a_3=S_3-S_2=12-5=7.

Step 4 · a_4=S_4-S_3=22-12=10.

Check · 1+4+7+10=22, which is S_4.

Answer · The original terms are 1, 4, 7, 10.

Example · evaluate a sigma sum

Question. Evaluate \displaystyle\sum_{n=4}^{7}(5n+1).

Quick fact: Start at the bottom number and stop at the top number. Put each whole number into the expression, then add the results. Here the counter is n, from 4 up to 7.

Step 1 · n=4: 5\times 4+1=21.

Step 2 · n=5: 5\times 5+1=26.

Step 3 · n=6: 5\times 6+1=31.

Step 4 · n=7: 5\times 7+1=36.

Step 5 · Add: 21+26=47, 47+31=78, 78+36=114.

Answer · \displaystyle\sum_{n=4}^{7}(5n+1)=114.

Example · write a sum with sigma

Question. Write 5+10+15+20+25+30+35 in sigma notation, starting at n=1. Do not evaluate it.

Quick fact: Find a rule that turns the counter n into the nth term. Then the bottom number is 1 and the top number is how many terms you have.

Step 1 · The terms are 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35. That is 7 terms.

Step 2 · Term 1 is 5\times 1. Term 2 is 5\times 2. Term 7 is 5\times 7.

Step 3 · So the nth term is 5n, and n runs from 1 to 7.

Answer · \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{7}5n.
