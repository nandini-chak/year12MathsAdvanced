13 · Exercise 1A

a
i Explain why the integers, written in increasing order, do not form a sequence.
ii How can all the integers be written as a sequence?
b Explain why the points in order on the closed interval [0, 1] do not form a sequence.
c Explain why the empty set is not a sequence.
d Can a sequence have a single term?

Quick fact: To find a term from a formula, substitute n=1,2,3,\ldots into a_n. To test if a value is a term, set a_n= (that value) and solve for n; it must be a whole number.

a · i A sequence must have a first term; however, the set of integers is infinite i.e. whichever term is chosen to begin with, there is another term that precedes it accordingly. ii Any sequence with a first term that captures a subset of the integers e.g. 0, 1, 2, 3, · · · and −1, −2, −3, · · · – the union of these mutually exclusive sets forms the integers.

b · The interval [0, 1] contains uncountably many points. Suppose that there is a_n increasing sequence 0, a2, a3, · · · , 1 and a2 > 0. Trivially, a\tfrac{2}{2} ∈(0, a2), but a\tfrac{2}{2} is not in the previous sequence. Hence, there cannot be a second term, making it impossible to form a sequence in the given domain.

c · The empty set has nothing in it. Since there are no numbers, there cannot be a first term, eliminating any possibility of a sequence.

d · Yes, it has a first term and the number of terms n in the sequence is 1.

15 · Exercise 1A

The Fibonacci sequence is defined recursively by F1 = 1, F2 = 1, Fn = Fn−1 + Fn−2, for n ≥3. Write out the first 12 terms of the sequence. Explain why every third term of the sequence is even and the rest are odd.

F1 = 1 F2 = 1 F3 = F1 + F2 = 1 + 1 = 2 F4 = F3 + F2 = 2 + 1 = 3 F5 = F4 + F3 = 3 + 2 = 5 F6 = 8 F7 = 13 F8 = 21 F9 = 34 F10 = 55 F11 = 89 F12 = 144 The sum of two odd integers is even, and the sum of an even and an odd integer is odd.
