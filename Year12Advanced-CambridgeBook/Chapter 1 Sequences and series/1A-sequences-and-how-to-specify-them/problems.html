13 · Exercise 1A

a i Explain why the integers, written in increasing order, do not form a sequence. ii How can all the integers be written as a sequence? b Explain why the points in order on the closed interval [0, 1] do not form a sequence. c Explain why the empty set is not a sequence. d Can a sequence have a single term?

a i A sequence must have a first term; however, the set of integers is infinite i.e. whichever term is chosen to begin with, there is another term that precedes it accordingly. ii Any sequence with a first term that captures a subset of the integers e.g. 0, 1, 2, 3, · · · and −1, −2, −3, · · · – the union of these mutually exclusive sets forms the integers. b The interval [0, 1] contains uncountably many points. Suppose that there is an increasing sequence 0, a2, a3, · · · , 1 and a2 > 0. Trivially, a2/2 ∈(0, a2), but a2/2 is not in the previous sequence. Hence, there cannot be a second term, making it impossible to form a sequence in the given domain. c The empty set has nothing in it. Since there are no numbers, there cannot be a first term, eliminating any possibility of a sequence. d Yes, it has a first term and the number of terms n in the sequence is 1.
