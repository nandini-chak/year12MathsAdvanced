Further graph transformations and modelling

This chapter is about changing a graph without starting again. Once you know the shape of a parent function, every stretch, flip and slide can be read straight from the equation — and then used to model real situations.

y = k f(a(x − b)) + c

Four letters, four effects. k and c act vertically; a and b act horizontally. Dilations and reflections happen before translations.

What that looks like

How the chapter builds

We start with transformations in general, using any parent function, so the language of dilation, reflection and translation is settled first. The trigonometric sections then apply exactly that language to sine, cosine and tangent — amplitude and centre from the vertical changes, period and phase from the horizontal ones — before we solve equations and model with the curves.
